Working with a coding agent instead of a notebook

How I worked. I built this site with Claude Code running locally (model: Opus 5.5) instead of writing pandas in a Jupyter notebook. I gave it one instruction: read the assignment on Canvas and complete it step by step. It wrote the code, the charts and the first drafts of this text; my part was approving, deciding and asking questions.

What went faster. All ten questions, the filters and the map came out of one session. The interactive parts (a shareable link, a map that filters every chart, dark mode, a phone layout) are web problems I could not have built from a notebook in that time.

What the agent got wrong. The first Q4 chart attached the campus names to the wrong bars: it showed “UNC Chapel Hill” with the largest increase while the written answer said Illinois. I did not spot this. The agent caught it when it compared its chart with a separate pandas result, and rebuilt the chart. Later, when I asked whether an agent may write this paragraph, it found that its own first draft had said I caught that bug, and rewrote it.

What I decided. I let it use my GitHub account, chose to leave my Part 1 site unchanged after its grade came out, and before submitting made it re-read the instructions and check every requirement; that check found this paragraph was too long.

Which I prefer. For building a site, the agent, by far. In a notebook I would understand each number because I typed it; here I had to ask the agent to explain its work, and it is fast and confident even when it is wrong. I would keep a notebook check beside it.

Data & methods

How every number was computed

Rules

  • Price scope. Analyses use sales with a sale_price from $10,000 to $5,000,000, inclusive (a “valid price”), which removes clear errors such as a $0 sale and a few very high sales. Q2 and Q3 use all rows where they say so, and Q9 counts all rows with a sale date.
  • Days on market is contract_date minus list_date, in days, computed only for rows that have both dates. A blank date is never treated as zero.
  • Missing values. Blank fields are left out of the calculation that needs them and are never filled in. Garage, basement and fireplace blanks are shown as “Unknown”, not “No”. Q6 uses only rows with a tax amount above $0 and below 10% of the sale price.
  • Groups. A campus area is the university column (11 values). A city is city and state together, such as “Champaign, IL” (18 values).
  • Dates. Year and month come from sale_date.
  • Statistics. Medians unless a question asks for a mean; for an even count the median is the average of the two middle values. Ties are reported together.
  • No deduplication. Rows are never removed because two sales share a street address; listing_id is the key.
  • Rounding. Prices are shown in whole dollars, other figures to one decimal (two for tax rates).

How the site works

The page loads data/site_data.json, a compact copy of the CSV made by scripts/build_site_data.py with every row and only the columns the site needs. All statistics are computed in your browser from those rows. No figure is typed in by hand.
